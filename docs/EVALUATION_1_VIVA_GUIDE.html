EDA findings must be based on the approved downloaded file. The following are required checks, not invented results. Replace every “to verify” item with measured evidence, tables, and plots.

How to explain an EDA finding

  1. State the measured result.
  2. Explain why it matters for modelling.
  3. State the preprocessing or modelling decision it caused.
  4. State how the decision will be validated.
Do not say: “There are no missing values” unless the notebook output proves it. Say: “The approved file was checked, and the missing-value table showed [verified result]. Therefore we [decision].”

8. Viva Questions and Answer Points

Use these questions for individual rehearsal. Answer in your own words, using verified project evidence rather than memorised general definitions.

1. What problem does your system solve?
State the obesity-risk category classification scenario, intended users, input information, target output, and educational limitation.
2. Why is this classification and not regression?
The target is a set of named categories in NObeyesdad, not a continuous numerical quantity.
3. Why did you select this dataset?
Explain target clarity, relevant feature groups, sufficient records, public accessibility, task complexity, and suitability for the proposed scenario.
4. What is your target variable?
NObeyesdad; explain its meaning and list the observed classes only after verifying the approved file.
5. How many rows and columns are present?
Give the measured result from the downloaded approved file. The proposal expectation is 20,758 rows and 18 columns, but this must be verified.
6. Which features are numerical and categorical?
Identify the groups from the verified schema and explain why their treatment differs.
7. What did your EDA reveal?
Give two or three measured findings, their supporting plots/tables, and the decisions they caused. Do not invent findings.
8. Did you find missing values or duplicate records?
Cite the actual counts and explain the selected handling policy.
9. How did you investigate outliers and invalid values?
Describe range checks, plots or summary statistics, and why an observation is treated as an error or legitimate value.
10. Is the target balanced?
Use verified class counts/percentages and explain whether stratification, class weights, or macro-averaged metrics may be needed later.
11. Why should id normally be excluded?
It is an identifier, not a meaningful subject characteristic, and can create spurious patterns or leakage.
12. What preprocessing will you apply?
Name only decisions supported by the verified data: missing-value handling, duplicate/invalid handling, encoding, scaling, feature engineering, and split strategy.
13. What is data leakage?
Information unavailable at prediction time, or information from validation/test data, incorrectly influencing training or preprocessing.
14. How will you prevent leakage?
Separate data before fitting transformations, fit preprocessing on training data only, exclude target-derived features, and keep the test set untouched until evaluation.
15. Why use a stratified split?
To preserve class proportions across training and test sets for a multi-class target, when the data and split strategy support it.
16. What is feature engineering?
Creating or transforming variables to represent useful information more effectively; explain the proposed feature, reason, and leakage check.
17. What was your personal contribution?
Name exact files, decisions, commits, reviews, evidence, and what you learned. Do not describe only the group's work.
18. What would you improve next?
Mention later model comparison, tuning, error analysis, backend/frontend integration, testing, and stronger validation, without claiming they are already complete.

9. Final Preparation Checklist

Seven days before viva

  • Read the approved dataset proposal and assignment requirements.
  • Run or inspect the data-understanding outputs and replace all pending values.
  • Review every preprocessing decision and its evidence.
  • Read all other members' work, not only your own branch.
  • Prepare a 60-second explanation of the problem and a 60-second explanation of your contribution.

Before entering the viva

  • Know the dataset source, target, feature groups, row count, column count, and verified quality results.
  • Know the reason for every cleaning, encoding, scaling, splitting, and feature decision.
  • Know at least two leakage risks and how the team prevents them.
  • Know the exact files and evidence you personally contributed.
  • Be honest about work that is planned rather than completed.

Answer method

Use evidence

“We checked X and observed Y. Because of that, we chose Z.”

Show understanding

Explain the reason, trade-off, limitation, and validation method in your own words.
